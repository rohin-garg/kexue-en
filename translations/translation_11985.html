From Parameter Symmetry to Effective Learning Rate

By 苏剑林 | October 27, 2026

Recently, an increasing number of papers and blogs have begun to analyze and guide model optimization based on parameter symmetry, such as Hyperball, HyperTransfer, and ELR. These works generally revolve around the exploration of the symmetry hypothesis . If we design optimizers to "conform" to the corresponding symmetries, it often makes the training process more robust and reduces the mental burden of hyperparameter tuning.

However, the symmetry hypothesis itself has some controversial aspects. How can we find a more reliable foundation for this path to allow it to proceed more reasonably and further? This article shares the author's reflections on this topic.

5. The parameter of the LM Head's RMS Norm forms such a parameter pair with the parameter matrix of the LM Head.

Points 3 and 4 are particularly noteworthy: here, the entire set of input matrices must be scaled together. Scaling only one of the matrices, while canceling out the scale change of that pair, would cause other parameters in the group to be scaled by an additional factor, and the forward result would usually still change. Furthermore, some joint symmetries can be refined to the vector or even channel level, indicating that scale redundancy is not uncommon in modern models; it just usually depends on the coordination between parameters.

Reflections on the Derivation

Reviewing the entire derivation chain, we were mainly trying to argue the following points: the norm is not important, AUS is more fundamental, and the equivalence between AUS and ELR. But if we question further, we find that the choice of observation objects—whether norm or AUS—is quite subjective, without explaining what the more fundamental expectation behind it is.

We know that every symmetry represents an invariant property of the model; that is, for forward calculation, the model before and after the transformation represents two mathematically identical functions. For training, we expect two different but forward-equivalent parameterizations to obtain exactly the same training trajectory. This would enhance the transferability of various hyperparameters and reduce the mental burden during training.
