Continuous Softmax Attention

By 苏剑林 | October 19, 2026

Softmax Attention is surely no stranger to everyone; it is a weighted mixture operator defined over a finite set of vectors. Recently, the paper 《Continuum Attention》 proposed a very interesting idea—continuizing attention. This reminds me of EFLA, which I introduced earlier in "Why does DeltaNet need L2 Normalization?", which also continuizes a discrete RNN and then re-discretizes it to obtain interesting results, similar to the earlier SSM series of work.

It seems that "continuization-discretization" is a tried-and-true path for exploration. So, what interesting results can we get from continuizing Softmax Attention? Let's appreciate it together.

Simple Experiment

depends simultaneously on and its adjacent values, which also means it cannot reuse existing Flash Attention. However, the implementation principle is universal, and the theoretical FLOPs are basically the same. I had K3 write a simple Kernel, and the speed was only about 1/4 of Softmax Attention, mainly because there are too many fragmented operations inside, making it difficult to accelerate, but we can still run it first.

The first experimental result is that although continuization introduces a local prior, it cannot completely replace positional encoding. In a full Full Attention architecture, continuous Softmax Attention without RoPE is significantly worse than standard Softmax Attention with RoPE; conversely, adding RoPE can bridge this gap.

Regarding training dynamics, when everyone uses RoPE, continuous Softmax Attention is significantly better than standard Softmax Attention in the early stages, but the gap gradually narrows, and by the end of training, the two basically overlap and the advantage disappears. It is speculated that the core reason is that natural language is not a true time series, so continuization does not bring substantial benefits. Perhaps for real time series, there would be a chance to measure an advantage.

I also conducted experiments switching to the continuous version midway through standard Softmax Attention training and found that the jump in Loss was not high, and it eventually converged to basically the same effect as the standard version.

Summary

This article briefly derives the continuous Softmax Attention, showing that its result is similar to adding a Short Conv operation to the Attention matrix. Although no advantage was found for this continuization in natural language tasks, it remains an interesting result to appreciate.
